Recognizing definitions in a text
The decoding of a written word or phrase is a very important aspect of the act of reading.Without
aecoding the important written woras or phrases, reading comprenension is Impossiole. This
explains why some readers can "read" but cannot understand what they are reading. Sometimes the
author offers the reader the definitions of difficult written words or phrases in context to faclitate
understanding. If the reader can recognize the definitions ana integrate what is read with their prior
knowleage, common sense, etc.， the woras or phrases can be easily decoded. Usually, definitions
may be developed in the following ways.
• A definition typically starts with the meaning of a term. The meaning is then explained with a
series of details.
• A definition may be developed through examples.
• A detinition may be developea through comparison ana contrast.
• A definition may be developed by describing how a certain event happens.
Take the sentences from Text A, for exampre.
Example 1
She was part of a hard-luck group of jobless people who called themselves “99ers”， because they
had exhausted the maximum 99 weeks of unemployment insurance benefits that they could claim.
（Para. 4）
Question: Who are the “99ers"？
Answer: The "99ers' are a hard-luck group of jobless people who have exhausted the maximum 99
weeks of unempioyment insurance benefits that they could claim.
The author provides the meaning of "99ers" by explaining how people became "99ers" in the crisis.
Example 2
Modest payments of unemployment benefits were a lifeline that enabled people who were out of
work to maintain at least an appearance of normalcy Keeping a roof over their heads, putting gas in
their cars, and paying electric and phone bills. （Para. 5）
Question: What does "lifeline" mean in this particular context？
Answer： "Lifeline" in the context means something that enables people who are out of work to
maintain at least a normal life.
The author here uses an attributive clause to explain what a "lifeline" means.
